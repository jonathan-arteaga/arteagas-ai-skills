# Voice modeling

Use examples as evidence, not as a phrase bank.

## Infer only durable signals

Build a short internal model from repeated patterns:

- **Directness:** how quickly the author reaches the point.
- **Cadence:** typical sentence length, variation, fragments, and paragraph size.
- **Formality:** contractions, greetings, sign-offs, jargon, and conversational language.
- **Reasoning:** whether the author leads with a recommendation, evidence, story, question, or context.
- **Structure:** prose versus bullets, heading density, and how calls to action appear.
- **Texture:** humor, warmth, skepticism, qualifiers, idioms, and punctuation habits.

Do not infer identity, biography, beliefs, or private facts that the examples do not establish.

## Weight the evidence

1. Prefer recent samples from the same channel and audience.
2. Prefer traits repeated across several samples.
3. Treat unusual phrases, typos, and one-off emotional moments as weak evidence.
4. Preserve the current message's purpose even when an older sample used a different format.

## Keep imitation bounded

- Recreate decisions, not sentences. Do not lift distinctive wording from a sample unless the user asks to reuse it.
- Do not add personal stories or opinions just because they would fit the voice.
- Do not exaggerate quirks until the result becomes a caricature.
- If the examples conflict, choose the traits best matched to the present audience and channel.

## Quick acceptance test

Before returning the draft, check:

1. Would the opening feel natural without any explanation from an assistant?
2. Does every personal claim come from the supplied context?
3. Could three generic phrases be replaced with details that only fit this message?
4. Did the draft preserve the author's directness and degree of formality?
5. Would removing the author's name make the piece interchangeable with generic AI copy? If yes, revise the most generic lines.
